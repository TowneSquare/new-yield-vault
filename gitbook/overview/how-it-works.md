# How it works

## The lifecycle of a deposit

```
1. You deposit           USDC ──▶ Vault ──▶ Manager
                                  (mints you shares)

2. Capital is deployed   Manager ──▶ offchain trading strategy
                                     (via whitelisted addresses)

3. Results are reported  offchain engine ──▶ Yield ──▶ Vault
                                              (share price moves)

4. You request an exit   requestRedeem() — shares burned, amount locked

5. Cooldown elapses      (1 day by default)

6. Capital returns       Manager ──▶ Vault
                                     (operator-triggered)

7. You collect           redeem() ──▶ USDC to you
```

Steps 3 and 6 depend on offchain actors. Everything else you control yourself.

## The three contracts

The protocol is three upgradeable contracts, each with one job.

### Vault (`townSqToken`)

The ERC-4626 token you interact with. It mints and burns shares, tracks redemption
requests, and maintains the accounting that determines the share price. It deliberately
does **not** hold assets — deposits pass straight through to the Manager.

Because of this, `totalAssets()` is a bookkeeping number (`virtualAssets` minus profit that
has not vested yet), not a balance you can verify by looking at the vault's token holdings.

### Manager

Custodies every deposited asset and deploys capital into the strategy. Two flows matter to
you:

- **Out:** an operator calls `execute()` to send capital to whitelisted destinations. This
  is how funds reach the offchain trading book.
- **In:** an operator calls `transferToVault()` to push assets back so redemptions can be
  paid.

The Manager also enforces an allowlist — capital can only be sent to addresses that
governance has explicitly whitelisted.

### Yield

Receives the offchain engine's profit and loss reports and applies them to the vault. It
handles profit vesting, loss application, and the treasury's performance fee.

## Where the yield comes from

Not from onchain lending or liquidity provision. Capital is transferred to an **offchain
trading counterparty**, and returns are generated by that counterparty's trading activity.

This has two consequences worth being explicit about:

1. **The strategy's performance is not visible onchain.** The vault has no view of the
   trading book. What you see is the reported result.
2. **Recovering capital depends on the counterparty performing.** Once assets leave via
   `execute()`, the enforcement is contractual and offchain, not onchain.

{% hint style="warning" %}
This is the single most important structural fact about the protocol. See
[Risks and trust assumptions](../risks/README.md).
{% endhint %}

## How results reach you

An offchain engine calls `distributeYield(amount, feeAmount, nonce, isProfit)` once per
epoch — at most roughly once every 22 hours by default.

- `amount` is the shareholders' slice.
- `feeAmount` is the treasury's slice, **on top of** `amount`, not carved out of it.
- `isProfit` sets the direction.

**On profit:** `amount` is released into the share price linearly over the vesting period
(7 days by default), rather than all at once. This prevents someone from depositing just
before a report and capturing yield they were not exposed to.

**On loss:** the loss applies immediately, with no vesting. Profit that has not vested yet
absorbs the loss first — if the loss is smaller than the unvested buffer, the share price
does not move at all.

Each report is bounded: by default no more than **0.5% of vault assets** per epoch, and
epochs must be at least ~22 hours apart. Both limits are governance-adjustable.
